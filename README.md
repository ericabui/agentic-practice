The river behind the old station runs slow in the morning, carrying mist and fallen leaves toward the valley. Commuters cross the iron bridge without looking down, already rehearsing the day ahead.

Mara kept a small workshop above the bakery, where she repaired clocks and radios for neighbors. Every shelf was labeled by hand, and every drawer held a different kind of spare spring or dial.

In the summer the town held a night market along the canal, with paper lanterns strung between the trees. Children traded stories for sweets, and musicians played until the lights burned low.

The library on Elm Street had one chair that no one else would sit in, worn soft by decades of readers. It faced the western window, perfect for afternoon chapters and quiet departures.

Beyond the hills, a lighthouse that no longer guided ships was kept lit by a keeper who liked the routine. He logged the weather each hour and waved at passing trains he could barely see.
